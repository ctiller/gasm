# 0002. `native_decide` Restricted to Exhaustive Finite Domains

## Status

Accepted, 2026-08-27. (PLAN.md D1.)

## Context

Pointwise gates were demonstrated to be gameable, not hypothetically but in this
codebase: a Spike5-Wasm target realization emitted a single, precomputed canned output
stream and legitimately passed an equivalence theorem instantiated only against the
input that stream was derived from. `native_decide`/`decide` on a single ground instance
or on a merely-sampled domain is adversarially unsound under [`0001`](0001-vision-and-insights.md)'s
premise that implementations are generated by an untrusted, creative process — an
implementation specialized to the examined points passes while being wrong everywhere
else.

## Decision

Adopt [Law 10](../REVIEW.md#law-10-kernel-checked-gates-the-nativedecide-restriction-exhaustive-finite-domains-only)
as the repository's binding rule: `native_decide`/`decide` may discharge a verification
obligation if and only if the proposition is universally quantified over its entire,
finite domain (all 256 byte values, all 16×16 register pairs, every constructor of an
input enum). Single-instance ground checks are regression tests, must carry the `_inst`
suffix, must live in designated test/regression modules, and must never be cited as
verification evidence. Infinite-domain claims require kernel-checked structural proof.

## Consequences

Every whole-program contract and every native-evaluation-discharged obligation in the
codebase is auditable against this rule; the axiom-level gate (`lake exe
check_gates_axioms`, [`0009`](0009-findings-become-gates.md)) is the mechanical
enforcement that makes violations build-failures rather than review findings. The
instruction registry roundtrip gate (Phase 1) is the exemplar exhaustive-finite-∀ shape
this decision endorses. Migrating the codebase's pointwise `*_inst` equivalence checks to
real universal proofs (Phase 4) is downstream work this decision obligates, tracked to
zero in the gate allowlist.

## Provenance

Owner-stated. The owner's own words: "this depends on real universal theorems, and so we
should eliminate single case native_decide (and possibly eliminate native_decide)." The
specific mechanism ratified here — restricting to exhaustive finite domains, the `_inst`
suffix convention, designated regression modules — is the coordinator's operationalization
of the owner's directive, not owner-specified in that level of detail.

## Open question

The owner's original directive raised two options, not one: "this depends on real
universal theorems, and so we should eliminate single case native_decide (and possibly
eliminate native_decide)." This ADR ratifies only the weaker option — restrict
`native_decide` to exhaustive finite domains. The stronger option in the owner's
parenthetical — eliminating `native_decide` entirely, including for exhaustive finite
domains — has **not been decided** either way and is not disposed of by this ADR.

Cost of the stronger option, if it were later adopted: every allowlist entry in
`scripts/gate_allowlist.txt` categorized `finite-forall` would need a kernel-checked
structural proof in place of native evaluation. At present that is 6 entries (see
`TCB.md` T1: "the native_decide surface yes (migrate 36 grandfathered entries)" — the
finite-forall entries are the exhaustive-domain subset of that surface, distinct from the
36 grandfathered pointwise entries Law 10 already schedules for migration): the 8-bit
reverse/involution check (256 values), the DEFLATE length-code bounds check (256 values),
the DEFLATE distance-code bounds check (32768 values), the Spike5 bit-reversal check (256
values), and the two Spike2 Fibonacci soundness theorems (finite range checks). These are
exhaustive-domain checks that only native evaluation currently discharges; eliminating
`native_decide` outright would require either a `decide`-only (kernel-reduction) proof for
each — which may not be tractable at the largest domain (32768 cases) — or a genuine
inductive/algebraic proof replacing brute-force enumeration entirely. This question should
be put back to the owner before any migration work assumes an answer either way.
